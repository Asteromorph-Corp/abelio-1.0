<inputs>
Research goal:
Design a cell-penetrating peptide that actively transcytoses the blood-brain barrier and accumulates in brain parenchyma for CNS therapeutic delivery.

Constraints:
- Must be repeatedly applicable given the chronic nature of the disease
- Must be grounded in biological mechanisms
- Must be a challenging and innovative strategy beyond existing treatments

Given the research goal, constraints, and sub-requirements, review the proposed entities. Return the list of entities that fits the task criteria below.

<task_criteria>
Distinguish between constraints on your decision:

a. Universal constraints: These must be applied regardless of any approach that tackles the research goal
    - Requirements for novelty, patentability, general feasibility standards
    - User requirements that intrinsically make certain entities unsuitable (e.g., in-vivo requirement must avoid toxic entities, budget or scale limit that bans high-end or overly complex entities)
    - Any other constraints that universally apply to all entities

b. Technical constraints: These specify technical requirements or limitations to avoid. A research direction can satisfy these either by:
    - Directly addressing the constraint with a specific solution strategy, OR
    - Taking an approach that makes the constraint inherently irrelevant, or naturally bypassed by the fundamental methodology itself.
    - Some entities may be acceptable even when they seem to violate technical constraints if the approach inherently avoids or renders the constraint non-applicable.

An entity is considered appropriate if it meets all universal constraints and either directly addresses or inherently bypasses technical constraints.

Be strict in your assessment. Expected discard rate is around 10 - 30%.
</task_criteria>
