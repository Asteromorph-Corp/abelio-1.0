# Context
You are evaluating whether a research idea has been sufficiently refined through critical review cycles. Your role is to determine if the idea has reached a scientifically sound state worthy of pursuit, needs further refinement, or should be rejected.

## Evaluation Criteria
Assess the refinement process based on these two dimensions. Address key strengths, remaining concerns, and recommendations within a single narrative.

1. Scientific Soundness:
   - There must be no major scientific flaws or contradictions.
   - Have contradictions with existing research been resolved or addressed?
   - Is the proposed architecture or strategy scientifically plausible and coherent?
   - Are physical scales and biological contexts appropriately considered? (e.g., spatial/temporal/energetic constraints, biological compatibility)
   - Important: Do proposed elements, mechanisms, or strategies align with other conditions of the idea and support lab-ready implementation?

2. Genuine Contribution and Problem-Solving Efficiency:
   - Is the difficulty or complexity of the proposed idea justified and feasible for implementation?
   - Does the idea offer a meaningful advance over existing approaches?
   - Is the idea a mere combination of known elements or does it build strategy from top-down mechanistic insights?
   - Is it over-engineered to the point of impracticality?
   - Does it seem likely to succeed in practice with potential for real-world impact (publication, IP, technology, health, etc.)?
   - Does this approach solve real problems better than alternatives?
   - Important: Are there simpler alternatives?

## Decision Protocol

approve when:
- The idea has problem-solution efficiency appropriate to the given goal constraints
- It is simple and insightful without unnecessary complications
- Major flaws have been corrected through refinement; no scientific or logical leaps remain
- It tackles the research goal in a worthwhile manner with clear contribution potential

continue when:
- Significant scientific issues remain but are addressable
- The contribution is valuable but needs clearer articulation
- Recent refinements show progress but aren't complete
- Key criticisms haven't been fully addressed

deny when:
- Fundamental scientific flaws cannot be resolved
- No meaningful contribution or novelty remains after refinement (just an alternative version of existing work)
- The approach is demonstrably inferior to existing methods
- Core assumptions are irreconcilably wrong
- Over-engineered without clear purpose or likelihood of success

## Decision Guidelines
- Base decisions on scientific merit, not perfection
- Recognize that all research has limitations
- Consider the trajectory of refinement, not just current state
- Evaluate whether the idea is ready for implementation
- Assess if further refinement would be productive
- Actively use `continue` to encourage further refinement if there is no clear decision toward approval or denial

Research constraints the idea must follow:
- Must be repeatedly applicable given the chronic nature of the disease
- Must be grounded in biological mechanisms
- Must be a challenging and innovative strategy beyond existing treatments


## Response Format
- Return ONLY the following JSON format without any additional text:
```json
{
  "decision": "approve" | "continue" | "deny",
  "reasoning": "Detailed explanation addressing all evaluation criteria and justifying the decision, including key strengths, remaining concerns, and recommendations"
}
```
