<task>
You will be given a novel research idea or an approach, and will be tasked with writing a research proposal in report format.

<workflow>
1. Identification of the Concept
  - Summarize the core proposed idea in a single paragraph
  - Focus exclusively on the idea itself
  - Do NOT include overly specific mechanisms or discuss further implications or expected outcomes
  - Write the 'novel idea' with a concise amount of scientific rationales
  - Keep it high-level yet scientifically grounded: the reader must instantly understand the idea without needing further explanation
  - This section is about delivering the novel idea itself: do not try to add any citations

2. Formulate Background
  - This is NOT about expected outcomes, impact, or defending the idea
  - Briefly showcase the background and context of previous works related to the topic
  - While introducing previous works (how they approached the problem), briefly show how our idea tackles the present limitations
  - Put less emphasis on mechanistic details: focus on the overall concept and results of the prior works
  - Must be contained in one concise paragraph

3. Revise Mechanisms
  - Understand mechanistic perspectives of the idea in detail
  - Then, perform extensive scientific reasoning and literature search to examine how the idea works at the molecular/technical level
  - Based on the understanding, demonstrate the idea in mechanistic level clearly and scientifically
  - Do not add new information or methodologies - never pass beyond information provided in the original input
    - You must think about how to 'deliver' the mechanism, not to modify or strengthen it
  - Strictly focus ONLY on explaining how it works - do NOT discuss novelty, innovation, differentiation from other methods, or any comparative statements
    - Remove all evaluative language such as "The innovation lies in...", "This differentiates from...", "The key advantage is..."
  - Write in a descriptive, explanatory mode: state what happens, how components interact, what molecular events occur, in what sequence
  - NO metaphors, analogies, or unnecessary verbose descriptions - be precise and technical
  - Use compact, dense academic prose: favor noun phrases over verbose clauses, employ technical terminology precisely
  - Organize content logically by mechanistic steps or components, using subsections if helpful
  - Use concise language and avoid repetition, while not sacrificing detailedness of the explanation

4. Write report
  - Write down and output the report based on the given <output_format>.
</workflow>

Structure as an English scientific report. Only output the report, and nothing else.

  - STRICTLY distinguish inferences and facts: do not use definitive language for predictions or inferences
